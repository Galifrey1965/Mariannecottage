The cottage

An 1800s longère, lived in slowly.

The longère is the long, low farmhouse of rural Normandy — built from local Pierre de Caen, lime-washed, oak beams worked by hand. We have restored ours sympathetically: the original character preserved, the comforts modern, nothing pretending to be older or newer than it is.

Inside, two bedrooms — one double, one twin — share a bathroom across a hallway worn smooth by two centuries of footsteps. There is a sitting room with a wood-burner, a breakfast table that catches the morning light, and books we have read more than once.

Outside, an orchard of Normandy apple trees, a garden the roe deer cross at dawn, and the church bell at the village ringing the hours. A stay here is unhurried by design. Read on the terrace. Walk the lanes. Eat breakfast we have made for you.

"At dawn, two roe deer cross the bottom of the orchard, the church bell rings six, and the whole valley is still asleep. This is the silence of the dairy country."

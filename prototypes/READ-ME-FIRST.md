# Marianne Cottage — three visual directions to look at

Hello Mark and Kim,

Inside this folder are three different design mockups for the cottage's website — three ways the same content could look and feel. They are **just visual designs** at this stage; they are not the live site, and nothing here is final. The goal is to find out which *direction* you both like best, so we can take that one forward and refine it.

It would be a huge help if you could **look at all three, talk between yourselves, and tell us which feels most like Marianne Cottage to you** — and why. Detailed feedback is welcome but not required; even "we both liked the second one best" is a useful answer.

---

## How to look at them

Each design is a single HTML file. Just **double-click** any of them and it will open in your normal web browser:

- `design-A-restyle.html`
- `design-G1-quiet-luxury.html`
- `design-G2-warm-story.html`

You don't need an internet connection for most of it (only the fonts come from online). Try resizing the browser window — they're built to work on phones too, so dragging the window narrow will show you the mobile version.

**Suggested viewing order:** G1 first, then G2, then A. That way you'll feel the difference between the two main contenders before seeing the more conservative option.

---

## What's the same in all three

To make the comparison fair, every design has:

- The same **content** — the same words, the same photos, the same nearby places (Cerisy Abbey, Omaha Beach, Bayeux Cathedral, Saint-Lô).
- The same **page structure** — header, hero photo, intro, "the cottage" section, photo gallery, "what's nearby", booking call-to-action, footer.
- The same **booking message** — "Book Direct — save 5%".
- The same **address, phone, email**.

So please don't focus on the words or the photos — those are placeholders we'll polish later. **Focus on the *feeling* each design gives you.**

## What you're really choosing between

The fonts and colours are just the surface. Underneath, these three designs are three different **postures** the cottage takes towards a guest. The deeper question is:

1. **How loud should the cottage be?** G1 whispers. G2 narrates with warmth. A talks at normal conversational volume.
2. **Cool or warm in temperature?** G1 is muted stone-and-sage (cool, restrained). G2 commits to terracotta and moss (warm, earthy — closer to the actual colour of Normandy stone and clay tile).
3. **Is the cottage a *place* or a *story*?** G1 says "this is what is here — see for yourself". G2 says "let me tell you about this place". A doesn't really pick — it presents information cleanly.

If you can tell us which side of those three questions feels right, the rest will follow.

## A fuller comparison

| Axis | A — Restyle | G1 — Quiet Luxury | G2 — Warm Story |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Posture** | Competent, modern | Confident, understated | Emotive, narrative |
| **Genre cousin** | A clean Squarespace template | Sawday's, Aman, a *Cereal* magazine spread | A long-form *Condé Nast Traveller* feature, a Kinfolk piece |
| **Palette** | Cool — sage + limestone + dark ink | Very muted, almost monochrome stone | Warm — terracotta + moss + paper |
| **Typography** | Inter + Fraunces (modern, slightly literary) | Cormorant Garamond (classical, ornamental italics) | Lora (bookish, narrative) |
| **Loudness** | Medium | Very quiet | Loud where it matters |
| **Photo style** | Rounded cards with soft shadows | Square corners, no chrome, photos as prints | Full-bleed cinematic, big hero, mixed-size grid |
| **Motion** | None | Almost none | Gentle fade-ins as you scroll |
| **Booking section** | Dark inverted card | Quiet centred band | Saturated terracotta full-bleed |
| **What it signals** | "This is well-run" | "This is curated" | "This is a place" |
| **Risks** | Could feel generic | Could feel cold or under-stated | Could feel performative if the photos aren't strong |
| **How much it depends on great photography** | Forgiving | Medium | High — the big photos will live or die by their quality |

## Things to ignore (we'll fix them later)

- The photos are placeholders from what's already on the website. Real cottage photography is a separate piece of work.
- "Book Direct — save 5%" buttons go to email — the proper booking flow doesn't exist yet.
- The Rooms / Gallery / Explore / Contact buttons in the menu don't go anywhere yet — these are designs of the **home page only**, not the whole site.
- The language switcher (EN / FR / DE) is decorative for now — translations come later.
- Small differences in spacing or wording — those are easy to tune once we've picked the direction.

## What we'd love to know from you

Even short answers are useful. Try these:

1. **Which of the three feels most like Marianne Cottage to you?** Just an instinct is fine.
2. **Which one feels *least* right?** Sometimes ruling things out is faster than picking a winner.
3. **Is the warmer palette (G2 — terracotta and moss) closer to the cottage's real character, or does the cooler stone-and-sage palette (G1) feel more right?**
4. **Anything that made either of you actively wince?** Aesthetically — fonts, colours, photo sizes, anything.
5. **Anything missing** that you'd want a guest to see on the home page that none of the three include?

You can reply by email, or just chat through it next time we speak — whatever's easiest.

Thank you for taking the time to look. The website is going to feel like *yours* by the end of this, and your taste is the final filter on every choice — so please don't hold back.
